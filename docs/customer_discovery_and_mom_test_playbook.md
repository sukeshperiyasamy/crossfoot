# Customer Discovery & The Mom Test Call Playbook
## 10–15 Problem Validation Conversations for B2B Data Consolidation & Reporting

The goal of these discovery conversations is **not** to pitch software, ask for compliments, or convince 10–15 business owners that dashboards are useful. It is to discover whether they already experience a **frequent, expensive, unresolved operational bottleneck**—and how they currently deal with it.

Using Rob Fitzpatrick’s **The Mom Test** principles, keep every conversation anchored in **specific past events, existing workarounds, actual hours/costs, and decisions they have already made**. Avoid compliments, feature pitches, and hypothetical buying questions (*"Would you buy a tool that...?"*).

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **The Golden Mom Test Rule:** The more specific and recent the story, the stronger the evidence.  
> * *"Month-end reporting is a headache"* = **Weak evidence** (polite platitude).  
> * *"Last month, two employees spent 14 hours matching Tally entries against bank credits, and ₹80,000 remained unexplained"* = **Strong evidence** (quantifiable pain).

## 1. The Conversation Opener (2-Sentence Disarming Script)

Drop their defensive guard immediately by stating upfront that you are conducting industry research and **not selling software subscriptions today**.

### Option A: For Factory Owners & Wholesale Distributors (Coimbatore, Karur, Chennai)
> *"Thanks for sparing 10 minutes—I’m not calling to sell you any software today. I’m mapping how growing manufacturing and distribution units in Tamil Nadu bridge the gap between daily shop-floor/godown realities and month-end Tally numbers, and since you run operations hands-on, I wanted to learn what actually works versus where manual work still wastes your team's time. Could I take 10 minutes to understand how this works in your business?"*

### Option B: For Chartered Accountant (CA) Managing Partners
> *"Thanks for giving me 10 minutes—I’m not pitching any software subscriptions today. I’m studying where CA teams lose the most unbillable hours when SME clients hand over messy spreadsheets, bank statements, or marketplace CSVs before audits and monthly closes, and I’d love to learn from what your practice sees firsthand. Could I ask a few questions about your client document workflows?"*

**Why this works:**
* Explicitly states *"I'm not selling software today"*, disarming sales resistance.
* Names familiar operational tasks (Tally, godown/shop-floor logs, bank statements).
* Allows the possibility that their process is fine (*"where, if anywhere, it gets difficult"*).
* Positions them as the expert operator.

## 2. Ten Non-Leading, High-Yield Discovery Questions

*Rule: Do not read all 10 like a rigid interrogation checklist. Pick the 5–6 best suited to their role and follow the thread wherever you hear frustration, pauses, or sighs.*

### Question 1: The Chronological Month-End Replay
> **"Walk me through how you prepared your most recent monthly report—from the very first step to the final number on your desk."**
* **What to Listen For:** The actual workflow—who collects files, which systems are involved (Tally, Excel, paper challans, WhatsApp), how many handoffs occur, and where manual data copying enters.
* **Mom Test Signal:** Specific past behavior vs. idealized routine.

### Question 2: Source Fragmentation & Data Handshakes
> **"What specific files, spreadsheets, or systems did your team have to pull together to finalize those numbers?"**
* **What to Listen For:** Number of workbooks, branches, bank statements, marketplace CSVs (Amazon/Shopify), physical stock logs, and duplicate data entry across files.
* **Mom Test Signal:** Workflow complexity, row volumes, and potential pipeline scope.

### Question 3: The "Two Numbers Disagree" Test
> **"Tell me about the last time two reports or records showed different numbers for the same thing—like physical stock vs. Tally, or dispatch logs vs. billed invoices."**
* **What to Listen For:** Concrete reconciliation friction. How was the mismatch discovered? How many hours did it take to trace? Was it a timing difference, a typo, or real money leakage?
* **Mom Test Signal:** Frequency, severity, and current workaround.

### Question 4: Spreadsheet Fragility & Key-Person Dependency
> **"When an Excel tracker or report breaks or causes confusion, what usually happens next—and what happens if the person who built it is on leave?"**
* **What to Listen For:** Broken formulas, overwritten cells, VLOOKUP crashes, multiple "final_v2" versions, and single-person dependency on an MIS clerk or junior accountant.
* **Mom Test Signal:** Operational fragility and business continuity risk.

### Question 5: Staff Hours & Time Costs
> **"Think back to the last month-end close. Which specific tasks took the longest, and roughly how many hours or days did your staff spend on them?"**
* **What to Listen For:** Concrete time burn. Chasing files, typing numbers, manual transaction matching, or correcting errors. Ask for actual hours rather than accepting *"it takes too long."*
* **Mom Test Signal:** Labor cost and opportunity cost of key personnel.

### Question 6: Business Decision Bottlenecks
> **"Can you recall a recent business decision that was delayed or compromised because a report or number was not ready in time?"**
* **What to Listen For:** Delayed supplier payments, missed safe-T claim windows, stalled purchasing decisions, slow debtor follow-up, or delayed board/partner meetings.
* **Mom Test Signal:** Tangible business impact and urgency.

### Question 7: Pre-Signoff Quality Control
> **"How do you currently verify that the figures are 100% correct before you or management rely on them?"**
* **What to Listen For:** Manual spot checks, founder Sunday-night reviews, cross-checking against bank balances, or blind trust without formal verification.
* **Mom Test Signal:** Trust deficit and anxiety around numbers.

### Question 8: Past Failed Fixes (The Ultimate Buying Signal)
> **"What have you or your team already tried to make this process easier or faster in the past?"**
* **What to Listen For:** Previous attempts: custom macros, hiring an extra MIS clerk, third-party software add-ons, or CA outsourcing.
* **Mom Test Signal:** If they have complained for 3 years but invested ₹0 and 0 hours to fix it, they are non-buyers. If they already tried 2 fixes and failed, they are prime buyers.

### Question 9: Stakeholder Ownership & Opportunity Cost
> **"When reporting bottlenecks happen, who gets dragged into fixing them—and what other work gets put on hold?"**
* **What to Listen For:** Does the owner, general manager, plant head, or senior partner get diverted from core revenue-generating work to fix spreadsheets?
* **Mom Test Signal:** Identifies the economic buyer vs. the daily user.

### Question 10: The Magic Wand Prioritization Close
> **"If you could snap your fingers and fix just ONE part of the reporting or reconciliation workflow you went through last month, which one would it be, and why?"**
* **What to Listen For:** Their unsolicited priority problem. Then probe: *"How long has that been an issue, and who else in your industry circle deals with it?"*
* **Mom Test Signal:** Priority level and warm peer referral opportunities.

## 4. Digging Deeper: How to Handle Surface-Level Deflections

When business owners give quick or defensive answers, do not argue. Probe with genuine operational curiosity:

| What They Say (Deflection) | What It Usually Means | Exact Follow-Up Probing Script |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **"Everything is fine, we don't have problems."** | Polite autopilot or saving face. | *"That’s great—most businesses your size struggle with month-end latency! How did you set it up so smoothly? When goods leave the warehouse today, how does that reach management reporting without manual entry?"* |
| **"My accountant / CA handles all of that."** | Confusing statutory tax filing with real-time operational MIS. | *"Got it—CAs handle tax and GST filings well. What about operational numbers your CA doesn't see, like job-work scrap rates, daily stock movement, or salesperson floor discounts? Who tracks those week-to-week?"* |
| **"We use Excel for everything."** | Excel is their default duct tape. | *"What specific files does the team open most often during month-end? And when you need to merge three of those files, who does it and how long does it take?"* |
| **"We reconcile everything inside Tally."** | True for core vouchers, false for multi-channel operations. | *"Understood. What records from outside Tally—like bank statements, gate pass registers, or courier CSVs—have to be cross-checked against Tally vouchers to be sure?"* |
| **"It takes time, but that's just normal in business."** | Normalized inefficiency. | *"How many hours or days did your staff spend on it last month? And what other work was delayed while they were working through those files?"* |
| **"We tried automating it before, but it failed."** | Previous bad experience with ERP/macros. | *"What specifically did you try? What happened when it hit your live data, and what made you stop using it? What would a solution actually need to do differently to work reliably?"* |

### The Five Follow-Up Probes to Memorize
1. **"Can you give me a recent example of that?"** *(Shifts from hypothetical opinion to factual evidence)*
2. **"What happened right after that?"** *(Reveals the sequence of events and failure points)*
3. **"How do you handle that today?"** *(Identifies their current manual workaround)*
4. **"Roughly how many hours or rupees did that cost you?"** *(Quantifies the impact)*
5. **"What have you tried so far to prevent it?"** *(Tests their willingness to invest in a solution)*

## 5. The "Paid Pilot" Pivot (Transitioning from Research to Business)

**Strict Rule:** If the prospect has no recurring pain, small data volume, or smooth operations, **do NOT pitch**. Thank them warmly, ask who else you should interview, and conclude the call.

Only pivot when they have described a **concrete, painful, recurring problem** (e.g., *"My staff wastes 4 days every month matching Tally against 8 godown sheets and we still find stock discrepancies"*).

### The 4-Step Ethical Transition Script

```mermaid
graph LR
    A[Step 1: Reflect Pain] --> B[Step 2: Ask Permission]
    B --> C[Step 3: State 4-Day Pilot Scope & Price]
    C --> D[Step 4: Low-Pressure Commitment Check]
```

#### Step 1: Reflect Their Specific Problem in Their Own Words
> *"Let me make sure I’ve understood you correctly: the main headache isn’t just compiling the report—it’s that two employees spend almost 4 days every month manually matching Tally sales against your warehouse dispatch logs, and unresolved discrepancies carry forward month after month. Did I capture that accurately, or did I miss something?"*

#### Step 2: Ask Permission to Break Frame
> *"Thank you—that’s very clear. I specialize in building deterministic Python and Excel pipelines that eliminate that exact reconciliation friction. I have a small, fixed-scope diagnostic process I run for a few select businesses to investigate these exact bottlenecks. Would it be alright if I took 60 seconds to explain how that works? No pressure at all if it's not relevant."*

#### Step 3: Present the Safe, Time-Boxed 4-Day Diagnostic Pilot
> *"The diagnostic is a 4-business-day sprint priced at a flat **₹8,500 INR** (or **$250 USD** for international clients). Here is exactly how it works:
> 1. We sign a strict **1-page Mutual NDA first**—your data is processed 100% locally and offline on my machine, with zero uploads to public AI models or cloud platforms.
> 2. You share just **two sample workbooks or CSV exports** from last month (up to 100,000 rows).
> 3. In 4 days, I deliver: (a) a complete Data Health & Reconciliation Audit, (b) a deduplicated, clean master dataset, (c) an automated Exception Tab flagging every mismatch or missing entry, and (d) a 1-page summary dashboard.
> 4. If you subsequently decide to proceed with a full implementation within 14 days, **100% of this ₹8,500 fee is credited directly** against that project."*

#### Step 4: Low-Pressure Commitment Check
> *"Would it be helpful if I emailed you the 1-page NDA and pilot scope today so you can see if it fits what we discussed?"*

## 6. Post-Call Discovery Scorecard (Grade Every Call in 2 Minutes)

Score each interview immediately after hanging up to filter out polite tire-kickers from high-urgency pilot buyers:

| Criteria | 2 Points (Strong Signal) | 1 Point (Moderate Signal) | 0 Points (No Signal) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1. Specific Recent Incident** | Cited concrete event from last 60 days with dates/SKUs | Described a general recurring issue without exact dates | General opinion or *"everything is fine"* |
| **2. Measurable Impact** | Quantified 15+ hours/month or specific ₹ financial exposure | Mentioned *"takes a lot of time"* without concrete numbers | Negligible impact or minor inconvenience |
| **3. Active Workaround** | Dedicated staff hours, complex Excel sheets, or manual checking | Informal spot-checks | No existing workaround |
| **4. Past Investment** | Tried software, hired MIS clerk, or built macros that failed | Looked into tools but took no action | Never attempted to solve it |
| **5. Stakeholder Authority** | Spoke directly with decision-maker (Owner/MD/Partner) | Spoke with influencer (MIS Lead / Accountant) | Spoke with end-user with zero budget authority |
| **6. Clear Next Step** | Requested NDA & Pilot SOW; agreed to sample file review | Agreed to follow-up call next week | *"Send your brochure / call me next quarter"* |

### Score Interpretation
* **9–12 Points (Prime Pilot Prospect):** High pain, active budget, clear authority. Send the [NDA and SOW Pack](file:///c:/Users/sukes/Downloads/business%20plan/legal_contract_pack_nda_and_sow.md) within 2 hours.
* **5–8 Points (Nurture / Research Insight):** Valid pain, but lacks urgency or authority. Extract their exact vocabulary for outbound copy; follow up in 30 days.
* **0–4 Points (Disqualified):** Low pain, no urgency, or polite brush-off. Thank them, ask for peer introductions, and move on.

## 7. How to Interpret the 10–15 Interview Set

After completing 10–15 calls, review your notes across all interviews and look for **cluster patterns**:

1. **The 3-Business Rule:** If at least 3 distinct businesses in the same industry (e.g., textile yarn distributors or CNC job-work units) describe the **exact same reconciliation failure** (e.g., job-work scrap leakage or unbilled delivery challans), you have found your **Flagship Offer**.
2. **Vocabulary Mining:** Extract the exact phrasing used by business owners (e.g., *"Sunday night stock check"*, *"godown bin card vs. Tally"*, *"courier weight slab overbilling"*) and embed those exact words into your:
   * [website.md](file:///c:/Users/sukes/Downloads/business%20plan/website.md) hero and problem sections.
   * [chatgptresult.md](file:///c:/Users/sukes/Downloads/business%20plan/chatgptresult.md) cold email and LinkedIn outreach templates.
3. **Execution Reality Check:** Do not build complex software or generic dashboards until you have confirmed that paying clients exist who will gladly pay ₹8,500 to see their own numbers reconciled.
